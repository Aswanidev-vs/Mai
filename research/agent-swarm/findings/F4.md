# F4: Challenges, limitations and open problems

## Findings

### [1] LLM-powered swarms incur ~300x computational overhead compared to classical swarm algorithms
- quote: "our LLM-based Boids simulation required roughly 300x more computation time than its classical counterpart, highlighting current limitations in applying LLM-driven swarms to real-time systems."
- url: https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.14496
- source_type: primary
- published: 2025-06-17
- confidence: high

### [2] LLMs significantly struggle with long-range planning and adaptive strategy under decentralization
- quote: "current LLMs significantly struggle with robust long-range planning and adaptive strategy formation under the uncertainty inherent in these decentralized scenarios."
- url: https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.04364
- source_type: primary
- published: 2025-05-07
- confidence: high

### [3] Cascading hallucinations propagate errors across agent chains, compounding costs
- quote: "multi-agent systems composed of multiple LLMs often suffer from inefficient task planning. This lack of structured coordination can lead to cascading hallucinations, where accumulated errors across agents result in suboptimal workflows and excessive computational costs."
- url: https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.19245
- source_type: primary
- published: 2024-10-24
- confidence: high

### [4] Hybrid LLM deployment can reduce multi-agent costs by ~89% but adds coordination complexity
- quote: "MaCTG significantly reduced operational costs by 89.09% compared to existing multi-agent frameworks, demonstrating its efficiency, scalability, and real-world applicability."
- url: https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.19245
- source_type: primary
- published: 2024-10-24
- confidence: high

### [5] Autonomous agents have higher costs and potential for compounding errors requiring sandboxed testing
- quote: "The autonomous nature of agents means higher costs, and the potential for compounding errors. We recommend extensive testing in sandboxed environments, along with the appropriate guardrails."
- url: https://www.anthropic.com/research/building-effective-agents
- source_type: primary
- published: 2024-12-19
- confidence: high

### [6] Imbalanced LLM abilities cause domination by superior models, undermining collaborative balance
- quote: "LLMs can effectively collaborate to reach a consensus despite noticeable inter-inconsistencies, but imbalances in their abilities can lead to domination by superior LLMs."
- url: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.11595
- source_type: primary
- published: 2023-05-19 (EMNLP 2023 Findings)
- confidence: high

### [7] Finite context length is a fundamental bottleneck limiting historical information and coordination quality
- quote: "The restricted context capacity limits the inclusion of historical information, detailed instructions, API call context, and responses... mechanisms like self-reflection to learn from past mistakes would benefit a lot from long or infinite context windows."
- url: https://lilianweng.github.io/posts/2023-06-23-agent/
- source_type: primary
- published: 2023-06-23
- confidence: high

### [8] LLMs struggle to adjust plans when facing unexpected errors, making them less robust than humans
- quote: "Planning over a lengthy history and effectively exploring the solution space remain challenging. LLMs struggle to adjust plans when faced with unexpected errors, making them less robust compared to humans who learn from trial and error."
- url: https://lilianweng.github.io/posts/2023-06-23-agent/
- source_type: primary
- published: 2023-06-23
- confidence: high

### [9] Natural language interface reliability issues force agent code to focus on output parsing rather than logic
- quote: "the reliability of model outputs is questionable, as LLMs may make formatting errors and occasionally exhibit rebellious behavior (e.g. refuse to follow an instruction). Consequently, much of the agent demo code focuses on parsing model output."
- url: https://lilianweng.github.io/posts/2023-06-23-agent/
- source_type: primary
- published: 2023-06-23
- confidence: high

### [10] Existing benchmarks do not fully capture unique challenges of decentralized coordination with incomplete information
- quote: "Existing benchmarks often do not fully capture the unique challenges of decentralized coordination when agents operate with incomplete spatio-temporal information."
- url: https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.04364
- source_type: primary
- published: 2025-05-07
- confidence: high

### [11] Frameworks create abstraction layers that obscure underlying prompts/responses, making debugging harder
- quote: "they often create extra layers of abstraction that can obscure the underlying prompts and responses, making them harder to debug. They can also make it tempting to add complexity when a simpler setup would suffice."
- url: https://www.anthropic.com/research/building-effective-agents
- source_type: primary
- published: 2024-12-19
- confidence: high

### [12] LLM evaluation of its own performance on expert domains is unreliable — the model cannot judge its own flaws
- quote: "while the LLM-based evaluation concluded that GPT-4 and ChemCrow perform nearly equivalently, human evaluations with experts... showed that ChemCrow outperforms GPT-4 by a large margin. This indicates a potential problem with using LLM to evaluate its own performance on domains that requires deep expertise."
- url: https://lilianweng.github.io/posts/2023-06-23-agent/
- source_type: primary
- published: 2023-06-23
- confidence: high
